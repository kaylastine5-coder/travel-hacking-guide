01 / How this works

This guide is meant to give you an intro to travel hacking and help you get started.

Not in the US?

I'm in the US, so that's what this guide covers. The concept is the same, but cards and some information might be different in other countries.

Overview

The quick overview of travel hacking is that you open credit cards to get the one-time big sign-up bonus. You then use those points to book airfare, hotels, or even rental cars. That's it.

  1. Open a cardfor the one-time big sign-up bonus
  2. Hit the minimum spendand you get your points
  3. Book your tripairfare, hotels, or even rental cars

What is a minimum spend?

The minimum spend is the amount of money you need to put on a new credit card within a set time to get the bonus, like $3,000 in 3 months. This is spending you're ALREADY doing, like groceries and utilities.

If you're a credit card user already, you can look at it like rather than earning 1 point per dollar spent and getting 3,000 points for $3,000 spending on your card, you can get up to 100,000 points for that same $3,000 spending by taking advantage of a single sign up bonus.

02 / Will this hurt my credit?

Opening a new credit card will ding your credit score by a few points initially, but that usually fades within a few months. As long as you are always paying your card off on time, and only open one card every three months, your credit score should be just fine. Showing you're a responsible credit card user actually HELPS your credit score.

Pay it off every month

This only works if you pay your card off in full every month. The interest will cost you more than your points are worth, so if you're carrying a balance, pay that down before you start.

The one exception is using a card to invest in a business. If your card has a 0% intro rate, it can be okay to make the minimum payments while you get your business up and running. Just know when the 0% ends, and have it paid off before then or get a 0% interest transfer card.

Some people also start their business on a card with interest. That's a business decision, not a points decision, so know your rate and have a plan to pay it off.

About to buy a house?

If you're about to buy a house though, do not open any cards until after you've closed.

Since you can open one card every three months, that means you and your spouse could open 8 cards a year between the two of you.

03 / Before you apply

I highly recommend that anyone starting out start with Chase credit cards due to their 5/24 rule.

What is the 5/24 rule?

The 5/24 rule means you can only open 5 credit cards within 24 months. If you try to open a 6th card in 24 months, Chase will deny you. Their 5 slots include ANY credit card, it doesn't have to just be a Chase card.

So say you applied for a Kohls credit card, an Amazon credit card, and a Chase freedom card in the last two years, (moving forward do not apply for any store credit cards, they are not worth it compared to the other offers out there) you've now used three of your five slots.

Before you start applying for cards, think about any cards you or your spouse have applied for in the past two years. Again, this could have been something as simple as an Old Navy card. They all count. You want to know where you stand with the 5/24 rule going into this.

The one caveat is that business credit cards do not count towards these spots, BUT, if you have filled all 5 spots, you won't be able to get a business card.

So start with Chase, and down the road move to Amex, Capital One or another credit card company.

Spouse tip

Don't add your spouse as an authorized user. This will take up one of their 5/24 spots.

What counts as a business

Business cards can get you SO far in the points and miles world. And the cool part is, anyone can get approved for them. There are tons of things that can count as a business in the credit card world, and the business card does not have to be exclusively for business purchases.

If you have an actual business, you can use that info. If you don't think you have a business, you might be wrong. All of these count as a business in the bank's eyes:

  • Selling things on facebook or ebay
  • Making money on the side
  • Running a blog or monetizing social media in any way
  • Babysitting, tutoring, or anything like that

You can also apply for a business you're just getting started, as long as everything you put on the application is true. All that to say, it's pretty much as simple as getting a personal card. If you don't have an LLC you would be considered a sole proprietor and can use your personal info for the business.

Stay consistent

Whatever info you use for a business card application, write it down so that you remember what you put for your next business card application.

05 / Hitting your minimum spend

Once you get your card in the mail, put ALL your spending on that card.

  1. 1

    Move your recurring donations to that card, utilities, groceries, business expenses, etc.

  2. 2

    Then when you hit the minimum spend, congrats! You get your points!

  3. 3

    Usually the points will post soon after your current statement closes and then you're free to use them.

  4. 4

    You can apply for your next card under your spouse's name and repeat the process.

Annual fees

If the card you're opening has an annual fee, that annual fee does NOT count towards your minimum spend.

Watch your returns

When you're putting spending on your card, pay attention to returns. You don't want to hit your goal and then later have to return something and now you're below the requirement. Because of this, I always like to go well past the minimum spend requirement.

07 / Using your points

Banks and travel partners

Your points live with the bank that gave you the card. You can book travel through the bank, or you can transfer your points to one of their travel partners, which are the airlines and hotels they work with.

Each bank has its own list of partners. Here are a few examples:

BankAirlinesHotels
ChaseUnited, Southwest, JetBlue, British AirwaysHyatt, Marriott, IHG
AmexDelta, JetBlue, British AirwaysHilton, Marriott

So if you want to fly Delta, you'd need Amex points. If you want to stay at a Hyatt, you'd need Chase points. That's why it helps to think about where you want to go before you pick a card, and then research which banks partner with those airlines and hotels.

To transfer Chase points, you'll need a card like the Sapphire Preferred. We keep ours and just pay the $95 annual fee each year so that we can transfer our points.

Before you transfer

Make sure the flight or hotel you want is available, because once points are transferred you can't move them back. Partners also change, so check your bank's website for the current list.

Travel vs. cash

You can absolutely redeem points for cash if you want. It's usually 1 point = 1 cent. If you want to travel though, you will get so much more for your money.

Do points expire?

It depends on the card. Chase and Amex points don't expire as long as your card stays open. Hotel and airline programs have their own rules. With an IHG card, you have to use the card once every 12 months to keep your points.

If you are going to close a Chase card, I would transfer the points to another one of your Chase cards before closing it. You can also easily transfer points with your spouse.

Point values differ

For example: 100,000 Chase points do not equal 100,000 IHG points (the IHG card is a Chase card, but you're earning IHG points and the values differ).

08 / Keeping or closing cards

Do NOT close cards unless you have to.

This actually hurts your credit score. I know some people don't like to have a ton of cards open, I get it. But after you've hit the minimum spend and have received your bonus, just stick the cards in a wallet in your closet or something and forget about them.

The only reason you should close a card is if it has an annual fee that you don't want to pay, or if you want to re-apply and get the sign-up bonus again. But even then, you could see if you could downgrade it rather than closing it. The Sapphire sign-up bonus is now once per lifetime. For other Chase cards the rules vary, so always read the offer terms before you re-apply.

If you're going to close a card

  • Wait until you've had it for at least one year. If you close prior to 12 months, you risk having your points clawed back.
  • If it has an annual fee, actually wait until the annual fee posts, and then call them within 30 days to close the card. They will refund you the fee.
  • You can also downgrade a card to a no annual fee card. Ask about this when you call.

09 / If you get denied

Don't give up if you get denied.

Down the road as you get more cards, you might have an application get denied. You can always call them to dispute it and they often just need a little more info and will then approve you. There are tons of articles out there about what you can do if this happens.

On the phone

If you're ever on the phone with Chase, or any bank, don't let them know you're a travel hacker. You just "like their credit cards" or something generic like that.
